#### [Where do Corporate Bond Funds invest?](#collapseThree-1)

As per the SEBI mandate, they need to invest at least 80% of their assets in AA+ and above rated corporate bonds and rest 20% can be invested in other debt and money market instruments including REITS.

#### [What is the investment tenure of Corporate Bond Funds?](#collapseFour-1)

Recommended investment tenure of Corporate Bond Funds is 3+ years.

#### [What is the risk profile of Corporate Bond Funds?](#collapseFive-1)

Corporate Bond Funds usually have Moderate risk profile. As these funds are required to take atleast 80% of their exposure in top rated corporate bonds, they are high on safety as compared with most other debt fund categories.

#### [Who can invest in Corporate Bond Fund?](#collapseSix-1)

*   Investors who want stable returns and want to avoid credit risks.
*   Investors who have moderate risk profiles.
*   Investors who have at least 2 - 3 years investment tenures
*   If you can remain invested for 3 plus years, you can enjoy benefits of long-term capital gains taxation

#### [Why invest in Corporate Bond Funds?](#collapseSeven-1)

*   Bank Fixed Deposit rates are on a declining trend. 3 to 5 year FD rates are in the range of 5.3 - 5.5% for leading public and private sector banks (source: bankbazaar.com). Interest rates may go down further in the future (data as on 31st Jan21).
*   Debt funds are subject to two risks - interest rate risk and credit risk. Interest rate risk is temporary, if your investment tenure matches with the duration of an accrual based fund. However, credit risk can cause a permanent loss.
*   Debt funds enjoy considerable taxation advantage over traditional fixed income investments, especially for investors in the higher tax brackets. Traditional fixed income investments like Bank FDs are taxed as per the income tax slab rate of the investor. Long term capital gains (investment tenure of 3 years or longer) in debt funds are taxed at 20% after allowing for indexation benefits. Indexation benefit can reduce your tax obligation considerably.

#### [What are the risks associated with Corporate Bond Funds?](#collapseEight-1)

*   Corporate Bond Funds are subject to market risk. There is no guarantee of capital safety and assurance of returns.
*   These funds can take duration calls. In case of adverse interest rate movements, fund NAVs can be volatile or even negative.
*   Though Corporate Bond Funds generally have low credit risks, credit downgrades can cause permanent reduction in fund’s NAV. One must always invest in debt funds which have high credit quality.

#### [What factors one should look in a Corporate Bond Fund before investing?](#collapseNine-1)

*   One should check the duration of the scheme portfolio and ensure that their investment tenure matches with the duration profile of the fund to avoid / limit interest rate risk.
*   One should check the rest 20% portfolio (Apart from the 80% invested in AA+ and above rated corporate bonds) for any credit risk.
*   Low expense ratio.
*   Exit load.
*   Track record of the fund manager
*   Track record of the Asset Management Company in managing Fixed Income Mutual Fund Schemes.

#### [What is the taxation in Corporate Bond Fund?](#collapseSixteen-1)

*   Short term capital gains (if the units are sold within 3 years) in debt mutual funds are taxed as per applicable tax rate of the investor.
*   Long term capital gains (if the units are held for more than 3 years) of debt funds are taxed at 20% with indexation.
*   Indexation benefits reduce the tax obligation considerably compared to investments in bank FDs and many small savings schemes. The first major advantage of debt mutual funds over bank FDs is regarding taxation during the tenure of the investment. In bank FDs, interest whether paid out or accrued in your FD account is taxable. The bank will deduct 10% TDS on the interest paid on FD.

#### [How Indexation works in Debt Mutual Funds?](#collapseSeventeen-1)

*   To calculate capital gains with indexation in debt funds, you should index your purchase cost by multiplying the purchase cost with the ratio of the cost of inflation index of the year of sale and cost of inflation index of the year of purchase, and then subtract the indexed purchasing cost from sales value.
