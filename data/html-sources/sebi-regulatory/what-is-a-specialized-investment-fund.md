# What is a Specialized Investment Fund (SIF)?

## Definition and purpose

SIF stands for **Specialized Investment Fund**. It is a SEBI-regulated investment product established by an eligible, registered mutual fund after SEBI approval. SEBI introduced SIFs to bridge the gap between conventional mutual funds and Portfolio Management Services (PMS): they remain within the mutual-fund regulatory framework while allowing more flexible and sophisticated portfolio strategies than regular mutual-fund schemes.

An investment product offered under a SIF is called an **investment strategy**, corresponding to a scheme under the mutual-fund framework. A SIF may offer equity-oriented, debt-oriented, or hybrid investment strategies. Depending on the permitted category, these strategies may use derivatives for limited unhedged short exposure in addition to hedging and portfolio rebalancing. This flexibility also makes SIFs relatively higher-risk products, with possible capital loss, liquidity risk, and market volatility.

## Who can invest?

For a non-accredited investor, the aggregate investment across all strategies of one SIF must generally be at least **INR 10 lakh at PAN level**. Investments in the AMC's regular mutual-fund schemes do not count toward this SIF threshold. The minimum does not apply to an accredited investor.

## How is a SIF different from a regular mutual fund?

- A SIF is not a PMS or an Alternative Investment Fund (AIF); it is regulated within India's mutual-fund framework.
- It is intended for investors who understand more complex, flexible, and relatively higher-risk strategies.
- It has a INR 10 lakh aggregate minimum investment threshold for non-accredited investors.
- It can use investment approaches, including permitted long-short strategies, that are more flexible than conventional mutual-fund schemes.
- The AMC must give the SIF a distinct identity and clearly separate its branding and offerings from its regular mutual fund.
