## SIF and Mutual Funds: Are They the Same?

A common question investors may have is: “If SIF is launched under the mutual fund regulatory framework, is it just another mutual fund?”

The simple answer is: **SIF is related to the mutual fund ecosystem, but it is not the same as a regular mutual fund scheme.**

SEBI’s framework states that an investment strategy under SIF means a scheme of a mutual fund launched under the Specialized Investment Fund. However, SIFs have a distinct framework, separate branding requirements, a higher minimum investment threshold, and more flexibility in strategy design compared with traditional mutual fund schemes.

## The Core Difference: Simplicity vs Specialization

Regular mutual funds are built for a wide range of investors. They may invest in equity, debt, hybrid assets, index strategies, or other SEBI-defined categories. Their appeal lies in simplicity, accessibility, professional management, and diversification.

SIFs are more specialized. They may follow strategies that are more flexible or complex than regular mutual funds. For example, some SIF strategies may use limited short exposure through derivatives, or may dynamically invest across asset classes such as equity, debt, REITs, InvITs, and permitted derivatives, depending on the strategy type and regulatory limits.

This flexibility does not mean guaranteed better outcomes. It only means the fund manager has a different set of tools to implement the stated strategy. Those tools can help manage certain risks, but they are also likely to add to complexity.

## Minimum Investment: A Big Difference

Many regular mutual fund schemes allow investors to start with relatively small amounts. SIFs, however, have a much higher minimum investment threshold. SEBI rules specify that the aggregate investment by an investor across all SIF strategies of the concerned SIF, at PAN level, should not be less than ₹10 lakh, except for accredited investors.

This makes SIF more relevant for investors with a larger investible surplus and a clearer understanding of risk. It may not be relevant as an entry-level product for someone taking their first steps into investing.

## Liquidity May Differ

In many open-ended mutual fund schemes, investors can usually redeem on any business day, subject to applicable rules and exit loads. SIF strategies may have different subscription and redemption frequencies depending on the nature of the strategy. A strategy could have daily, weekly, fortnightly, monthly, quarterly, annual, fixed maturity, or other suitable intervals. SEBI also permits subscription and redemption frequency to be different for a strategy, and notice periods may apply in some cases.

For investors, this means liquidity should be checked carefully before investing. The question is not only “What is the strategy?” but also “When and how can I redeem?”

## Risk Disclosure Is Important

Both mutual funds and SIFs are market-linked. However, SIFs may involve relatively higher risk due to the nature of strategies and instruments used. SEBI requires SIFs to show a “Risk-band,” with five levels ranging from the lowest to the highest risk. The risk-band is evaluated monthly and disclosed on the SIF/AMC and AMFI websites.

This is useful for investors, but it should not be the only factor. Investors should also read the full strategy document, understand the investment objective, check asset allocation, and evaluate whether the product matches their time horizon and risk appetite.

## Which One Should an Investor Choose?

The answer depends on the investor’s goals, knowledge, risk tolerance, and investment size. Regular mutual funds may be suitable for many investors who want accessible, diversified, professionally managed investments. SIFs may be more relevant for experienced investors who understand market-linked risks and can evaluate specialized strategies.

## Conclusion

SIFs and mutual funds are not competitors in a simple “better or worse” sense. They serve different needs. A regular mutual fund may be a starting point for many investors, while an SIF may be considered only after understanding its strategy, risk, liquidity, and suitability.
